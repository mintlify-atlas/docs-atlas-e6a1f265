Every expense you record in GastoClaro belongs to a category. Categories are the foundation of the app's analysis: they let you see not just how much you spend in total, but *where* that money goes. Without categories, a monthly total is just a number — with them, you can start to understand your spending patterns and make informed decisions.

## Available categories

GastoClaro ships with three core categories that cover the most common everyday expenses. You select one of these each time you add an expense.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Comida / Food" icon="utensils">
    Use this category for anything related to eating and drinking — groceries, restaurant meals, takeout, coffee, snacks, and work lunches. Food is typically one of the largest and most variable spending areas for most people, so tracking it consistently gives you meaningful data quickly.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Transporte / Transport" icon="bus">
    Use this category for costs associated with getting around — public transit fares, fuel, taxi and rideshare trips, parking fees, and vehicle maintenance. Transport costs tend to be regular and predictable, making them a good benchmark for comparing months.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Ocio / Leisure" icon="ticket">
    Use this category for discretionary spending on enjoyment and entertainment — cinema tickets, streaming subscriptions, sports activities, hobby supplies, concerts, and eating out for pleasure rather than necessity. Leisure spending is often where people find the most room to adjust their habits once they can see the numbers clearly.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

<Info>
  The MVP includes these three categories as a starting point. Future versions of GastoClaro plan to introduce custom categories so you can tailor the classification system to your personal spending patterns.
</Info>

## Why categories matter

Looking at a raw total — "I spent €800 this month" — tells you very little on its own. Breaking that number into categories reveals the story behind it:

- If **food** is unexpectedly high, you might identify that takeout has crept up.
- If **transport** spikes one month, you can trace it to a specific trip or fare increase.
- If **leisure** is higher than expected, you have concrete data to decide whether that reflects a choice you are comfortable with or a habit to reconsider.

GastoClaro's category chart on the main page visualizes this breakdown automatically. See [Data visualization](/features/data-visualization) for details on how the chart works.

## Choosing a category when registering an expense

When you fill in the expense form, the category field is a dropdown. Select the category that best matches the nature of the expense before you submit.

<Tip>
  If an expense could fit more than one category — for example, a work lunch that is also a social occasion — choose the category that reflects your *primary reason* for the spending. Consistency matters more than perfect classification: as long as you apply the same logic every time, the trends in your data will be reliable.
</Tip>

## How categories connect to the chart

Every expense you add is stored with its category label. The category breakdown chart on the main page reads this data and draws a proportional visualization showing what share of your total spending each category represents for the current month. When you add or delete an expense, the chart updates immediately to reflect the new distribution.
